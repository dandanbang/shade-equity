# 執行面研究：別的城市怎麼把樹種活、台北誰負責哪一步（2026-10-13）
Goal: a plan Taipei can actually carry out. Every claim has a URL. **[2nd]** = secondary summary or press, check it before quoting. **[gap]** = searched, not found.

## 1. 成敗經驗：各城市的數字
**Los Angeles: Million Trees LA (2006–) → City Plants**
- Fell far short of 1M trees. Of 91,786 trees planted 2006–10, five-year survival was **79.8% for street trees, 90.7% for park trees, 77.1% for yard trees**. The study names stewardship and follow-up visits as the critical factors. https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/40/5/286 (McPherson 2014, peer-reviewed)
- Implementation relied on many nonprofits and departments that had never worked together, and the city became a coordinator with no General Fund money. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2819482 (Pincetl 2010) · https://la2050.org/ideas/2013/million-trees-la-plant-toward-a-sustainable-urban-forest-that-supports-healthy-families
- **Giveaways inflated the count.** In 2007 nobody knew how many of the trees handed out at fairs were ever planted: https://planetizen.com/node/27263. A 2023 Controller report found only 19,851 new street trees, against 47,966 trees given away for private land **[2nd]**: https://www.torched.la/the-best-time-to-plant-a-tree/
- **Maintenance gap.** The LA Controller found street trees trimmed on a **14–18-year cycle** (5 years recommended). Emergency work costs **2.5×** proactive work, and there was no citywide inventory. The main recommendation was an online tree inventory. https://controller.lacity.gov/audits/turning-over-a-new-leaf-l-a-s-tree-trimming-and-maintenance-program (summary via search; page 403)
- In LA County, CAL FIRE grants that relied **only on residents to water** saw **20–25% mortality** against the 10% expected. TreePeople guarantees **3 years** of watering (15 gal/week). https://grist.org/drought/cities-want-more-trees-drought-is-complicating-their-efforts/ [2nd]

**New York: MillionTreesNYC (2007–15), TreesCount!**
- 1M trees planted. The mayor also funded **156 extra Parks staff + US$4.6M** in new maintenance money. In parkland plantings, **88%** survived year 1 and **90%** of those survived year 2. Volunteer-planted and staff-planted trees survived equally well (58% of trees were planted at volunteer events). https://www.nycgovparks.org/pagefiles/208/MillionTreesNYC-Report-Final__681a2a80851fe.pdf
- **Street trees** (Lu et al. 2010, n=13,405): 74.3% alive. By land use: 82.7% in 1–2-family residential areas vs 60.3–62.9% in industrial/vacant areas. **Stewardship index** (signage, plantings in the pit, mulch, weeding): trees with ≥1 sign were **91.6–100%** alive, trees with none **71.2%**. Missing trees were more likely on sidewalks under 5 ft. https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/37294.pdf
- Meta-analysis: street trees die at **3.5–5.1% a year**, so half of a cohort is gone in **13–20 years**. Most deaths come in the first 5 years. https://earth.sas.upenn.edu/content/street-tree-survival-rates-meta-analysis-previous-studies-and-application-field-survey · https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2019/nrs_2019_hilbert_001.pdf
- **TreesCount! 2015**: volunteers mapped **666,134** street trees, and every record went into the **official Parks forestry database**. Location accuracy was 96.1%. https://www.citizenscience.gov/catalog/217 · https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/44/2/49
- Request channel: residents ask via 311 or online and get a tracking ID. A forester checks the site for conflicts. Inspection can take **≥1 year**, then planting **≥2 years**. https://prd2.nycgovparks.org/trees/street-tree-planting/request
- Stewarded nonprofit programs reached **95.4–99.4%** establishment survival (Roman et al. 2015). https://canopy.org/wp-content/uploads/Roman-et-al-2015-article.pdf

**Phoenix: Tree & Shade Master Plan (2010) → Shade Phoenix Plan (Nov 2024)**
- The 2010 goal of 25% canopy by 2030 remained far from reached. The new plan drops the canopy-% target and instead sets **37 actions across 26 departments**, aiming for **27,000 trees and 550 shade structures** for US$60M. https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/phoenix-shade-plan-built-tree-canopy-extreme-heat/736789/
- Equity rule: **>50%** of investment to low/moderate-income areas, **85%** to low/moderate/middle. (Our shade-equity.md says ">90%". Use the smartcitiesdive figure.) The plan requires a new citywide inventory (2025) and **annual progress reports**. Council members asked for maintenance that is less siloed. https://phoenix.gov/heatsite/Documents/Heat%20Response%20Weekly%20Reports/Shade%20Phoenix%20Plan_Draft_CouncilVersion_June11%201.pdf [2nd via search]
- Engagement: talks to community groups, input at **budget hearings**, bilingual charrettes, and a survey. https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/fc03d8a6a86e4f998169205dc8705e56
- Tree Equity Score (TES) is used **as a map to pick priority areas**, not as a budget formula. Grants: schools up to US$75k; neighborhoods up to 200 trees + US$2k. https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2023/10/12/phoenix-funding-trees-shade-structures-underserved-communities/ Council later added **US$6M** on top of an initial US$1.6M. https://www.aztechcouncil.org/phoenix-gets-10-million-grant-to-plant-more-trees-cool-the-city-and-fight-the-heat/ [2nd]
- In the community tree program, **residents do the watering**; the city supplies a timer, a moisture meter and a hose. The city had no survival target yet. Same Next City source [2nd]: https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/phoenix-new-shade-plan-bus-shelters-trees-extreme-heat

**Melbourne: Urban Forest Strategy (2012)**
- Public-realm canopy **22% → 40% by 2040**. That needs **≥3,000 trees a year** plus footpath extensions and planting in the carriageway. Diversity rule: **≤5% any one species, ≤10% any genus, ≤20% any family**. The city warned that >20% of trees would be lost within 10 years. https://www.biophiliccities.org/tree-toolkit-melbourne · https://aiph.org/green-city/guidelines/case-studies/surveying-melbournes-trees/
- **10 precinct plans** give every project a delivery window: years 1–4, 5–7 or 8–10. Each is co-designed with residents through pop-ups, surveys and workshops. Kensington: 200+ participants; **72%** agreed the draft vision matched their hopes. https://participate.melbourne.vic.gov.au/kensington-urban-forest/community-consultation
- **Urban Forest Visual**: every public tree is mapped with its life expectancy and has an email address; thousands of emails were sent. Data is open (CC-BY). https://goodmenproject.com/featured-content/loving-emails-show-theres-more-to-trees-than-ecosystem-services-wcz/ · https://discover.data.vic.gov.au/en_AU/dataset/trees-with-species-and-dimensions-urban-forest
- **[gap]** No published canopy progress readout found.

**Singapore: NParks / OneMillionTrees (2020–)**
- **>500,000 trees planted with 75,000+ residents** (Apr 2023); expected to finish in 2027. Residents also help design planting plans and propagate saplings. https://nparks.gov.sg/news/2023/4/more-than-half-a-million-trees-planted-with-the-community
- One agency owns the whole cycle. **NParks' own certified arborists** inspect every 6–24 months, depending on location and size (major roads every 6–12 months). Inspection is not outsourced. https://mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/speeches/view/oral-answer-by-ministry-of-national-development-on-nparks-current-tree-inspection-regime · https://mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/speeches/view/written-answer-by-ministry-of-national-development-on-inspection-of-trees-under-nparks-purview
- Funding: Plant-A-Tree donations through the Garden City Fund. AIA pledged S$5M for 16,666 trees (≈S$300/tree). https://www.aia.com.sg/en/about-aia/media-centre/press-releases/2021/aia-singapore-pledges-five-million-to-support-nparks-onemilliontrees-movement
- **[gap]** No public site-selection criteria or maintenance contract terms found.

**Paris · Barcelona · Medellín · Seoul**
- **Paris**: the city says 213,000 trees in 10 years. Its 250-person tree service checks every tree yearly and replaces 2,000–3,000 trees a year. https://www.paris.fr/pages/dix-ans-de-plantations-a-paris-213-000-arbres-pour-une-ville-plus-resiliente-31389 · https://www.paris.fr/pages/comment-les-arbres-sont-ils-entretenus-a-paris-27125 Critics say Miyawaki saplings may mostly die, and the open data lags the official counts **[2nd]**: https://reporterre.net/Des-arbres-par-milliers-Paris-peine-a-tenir-sa-promesse · **[gap]** no audited survival rate.
- **Barcelona**: the tree plan 2017–37 was drafted in **54 participation sessions with 700+ professionals**. It aims for 25→30% canopy and ≤15% of trees from any one species. About 500 trees died in the 2023 drought under watering limits. https://www.sostenible.cat/print/article/arbres-per-viure-a-barcelona · https://beteve.cat/medi-ambient/moren-500-arbres-barcelona-sequera/
- **Medellín**: green corridors cost US$16.3M to build and US$625k/yr to maintain. Maintenance is done by gardeners hired from vulnerable groups (150, later 300+). **[2nd]** https://www.eldiario.ec/mundo/como-medellin-logro-bajar-su-temperatura-con-una-inversion-de-solo-650-dolares-por-habitante-24012026/ · https://www.elcolombiano.com/medellin/zonas-verdes-medellin-IB25063865
- **Seoul**: 30M-tree goal with ₩480B over 4 years. In 2020 the city shifted **from counts to species and location** (113 recommended species, a planting map). Redevelopment removed or transplanted ~7,000 street trees in 2 years. **[gap]** no survival data. https://news.seoul.go.kr/env/archives/501314 · https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2007517

**Why residents say no.** In Detroit, about **25%** of eligible households (1,800 of 7,425) refused free street trees. The causes were bad past experience with dead trees the city never maintained, and distrust of city government. Residents wanted a say in species and placement. https://www.uvm.edu/news/gund/why-people-reject-city-trees · https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/01/190107142109.htm. Philadelphia's plan engaged 9,000+ residents and made "reduce the burden of trees on residents" one of its 8 focus areas. https://www.phila.gov/media/20230223005617/Philly-Tree-Plan.pdf

**Recurring mechanisms (all cities)**
1. **Aftercare decides survival.** Most deaths come in years 1–5. Watering for 2–3 years by a paid crew or an organized steward helps; residents alone do worse (LA, Lu, Roman).
2. **Site quality.** Pit size, sidewalk width and land use matter (Lu). The space under the sidewalk has to be designed in (Melbourne footpath extensions, Singapore structural soil).
3. **Species diversity rules** (Melbourne 5/10/20; Barcelona ≤15%).
4. **Community input on *where* and *what*** before planting (Detroit refusals; Melbourne precinct co-design).
5. **One accountable owner plus an inventory plus annual public reports** (Singapore; Phoenix 2024; LA audit).
6. **Count trees alive, not trees handed out** (LA giveaways, Paris saplings, Seoul's shift).

**Taiwan failure modes (evidence)**
- 吳晟 (opinion, 2024): pits about 1 m wide and shallow with gravel; **1-year warranty**, after which no one is responsible; heavy pruning by untrained crews; species named in tenders inflate prices (**~NT$10,000 per 10 cm tree**). In 2015 Typhoon Soudelor damaged >10,000 of Taipei's ~90,000 trees. Tainan has **4,610 empty pits** out of about 38,000 trees. **[2nd/opinion]** https://www.twreporter.org/a/opinion-street-trees
- 新北 audit (2025): some species carry root-lifting and wind risk. Experts say the cause is site and maintenance, plus leaking pipes and bad excavation. 新北 is now running health checks on all 70,000+ trees; a councilor proposed a per-tree record (樹木履歷). [2nd] https://udn.com/news/story/7323/8960257
- 湖口 (新竹縣): **600+ of ~1,200** street trees dead. Replanted trees died again after the warranty ended. https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/HsinchuCounty/paper/1298862 [2nd]
- 台中: a QR-code 「行道樹電子身分證」 after a survey of 29,000 trees. Compensation table gives a base price ×2/×3/×6 by damage; e.g. 茄苳 ≥35 cm DBH = NT$35,000. https://www.dongshi.taichung.gov.tw/media/153411/7101316331471.pdf

## 3. 對應表：證實有效的機制 → 台北窗口 → app 要產出什麼

| Forester site check before planting (NYC); delivery windows (Melbourne) | 公園處 現勘 / work order | **施工清單 CSV**: pit ID (TaipeiTreeHole), TWD97 coordinates, sidewalk width, pit size vs 1.2 m, nearest pole, Street View link, citizen check result, suggested 1–4 / 5–7-year window |
| Utilities and digging conflicts | 新工處 道管中心, 3-year no-dig rule | Flag each site "on a sidewalk renewed in the last 3 years?" and **pair plantings with scheduled 新工處 sidewalk projects** (dig once) |

## 4. Implications for the app
**Build, ranked by how much each raises the odds the plan gets implemented:**
1. **Agency-ready site list** (施工清單 in 公園處 work-order fields, with the pit-size and utility screen). Evidence: inspection was NYC's bottleneck (≥1 year); 公園處's 1.2 m pit standard; Lu: narrow sidewalks lose trees. This cuts the agency's work, which is what moves a project.
2. **Survival tracker, not a planting counter.** Show planted / alive at 1–3 years / net change per 里. Evidence: Taipei's current fight is 13,225 vs 1,197 (counting); LA giveaways; Phoenix now requires annual reports.
3. **Stewardship: adoption plus watering reminders** through the existing 認養 rules. Evidence: trees with any stewardship sign 91.6–100% alive vs 71.2% without (Lu); stewarded programs 95–99% (Roman). Design it to **supplement** city watering, not replace it: resident-only watering gave 20–25% mortality.
4. **里長 one-pager + 「市長與里長有約」 proposal text.** Evidence: a real channel with 1–2-month tracking; Detroit refusals came from having no say; Melbourne precinct co-design.
5. **Citizen check → official inventory.** Evidence: TreesCount fed 666k records into the city database at 96% location accuracy; our Street View check found only ~1/7 of "empty" pits truly empty.
6. **Dig-once pairing with 新工處 sidewalk projects.** Evidence: the 3-year no-dig rule; the 1,350 km sidewalk program; Melbourne needed footpath works to reach its target.
7. **Councilor / participatory-budget packet.** Evidence: Phoenix council added money at budget hearings. PB alone is too small to move policy, so treat it as a visibility tool.

**Do NOT build**
- **A "trees given away / planted" leaderboard or million-tree counter.** LA missed its target and counted giveaways; Seoul and Phoenix both moved off count and canopy-% targets.
- **A residents-water-it-alone or free-seedling flow.** Resident-only watering had 20–25% mortality (LA County). Detroit residents refused because they expected to inherit the care burden.
- **Bulk 1999 complaint generation for planting.** 1999 dispatches 48-hour hazard work, not planting (inference: mass tickets would be misrouted and erode trust with 公園處).
